Privacy Policy — The Move

Last updated: 10 October 2026

The Move is operated by The Move.app LLC ("we", "us"). This policy explains what we collect, why, and what you can do about it.

Using the app without an account

You can browse venues, wait times, and the map without creating an account. While you do, we do not ask for your name, email, or date of birth. We record which screens you open using a random identifier stored on your device, which is not linked to who you are.

If you have already allowed The Move to use your location, the app uses it to pick the nearest city to show you. That choice is worked out on your phone; your location is not sent to us for it.

Features that involve other people or your own activity — checking in, reporting a line, following venues, posting, friends, and messages — need an account.

What we collect

Account information. Your email address, name, username, and — if you add them — a profile photo and bio. Email is used to sign you in and to contact you about your account.

Signing in with Apple or Google. If you sign in with Apple or Google instead of an email and password, we receive your name and email address from them. With Sign in with Apple you can choose to hide your email, in which case we receive a private relay address from Apple instead. We never receive your Apple or Google password.

Date of birth, city, and preferences. When you first open the app, we ask for your date of birth, the city you go out in, and the kinds of places you like — for example "Dive", "Live music", or "Brunch". Your date of birth is used only to confirm that you are 18 or older, and it is never shown to anyone. Your city and preferences are used to choose which venues you follow when you start, so your feed is not empty. Your date of birth is required; your preferences are optional, and you can unfollow any venue at any time.

Location. When you check in at a venue or report a line, we ask for your device location and check that you are within 125 metres of that venue. This is what keeps wait times honest — it stops people reporting on places they are not at.

If you are checked in somewhere, the app checks your location again when you next open it and checks you out if you have left. It also uses your location, if you have allowed it, to pick the nearest city to show you — worked out on your phone, not sent to us.

We only request location while you are using the app. We do not track you in the background.

The coordinates attached to a check-in or report are deleted automatically within a short period. We keep the fact that you were at a venue; we do not keep a history of where your phone has been.

Photos. When you report a line, the app takes a photo of the line and, optionally, a front-facing photo of you. Photos you post to stories, and your profile photo, are also stored.

Camera and photo library. The camera is used only when you take a photo in the app. When you choose a photo for your profile or a story, the app receives only the photo you pick — it cannot see the rest of your library. If you save one of your own photos to your phone, the app asks only for permission to add photos, not to read them.

Content you create. Posts, comments, reactions, direct messages, check-ins, line reports, the venues you follow or bookmark, and the points you earn.

Safety reports and blocks. If you report content or a person, we store the report, the reason you gave, and a copy of what was reported. If you block someone, we store that so they stay hidden from you.

Friends and connections. Who you are friends with, friend requests, and your visibility settings.

Device information for notifications. A push notification token, so we can send you alerts you have opted into. You control which notifications you receive in Settings. Check-in reminders after you report a line are scheduled on your phone and are not sent through our servers.

Usage analytics. We record which screens you open and which actions you take, to understand how the app is used. Before you sign in this uses a random identifier stored on your device; once you sign in it is linked to your account and email address. We do not use your network address to work out where you are for analytics.

Before you have an account

You can join our waitlist without creating an account. If you do, we store your email address so we can tell you when a place opens up.

Waitlist members get a referral link. If you share yours and someone taps it, we record that a tap happened — the referral code, the time, your browser type, and a scrambled version of the visitor's network address. The scrambling is one-way: we cannot turn it back into an address, and we use it only to stop one person inflating their own referral count.

We do not know who tapped the link unless they go on to join the waitlist themselves.

What we do not collect

We do not collect your contacts, your background location, your browsing outside the app, the rest of your photo library, or payment information. We do not sell or share your data for advertising, and we do not track you across other companies' apps or websites.

Who else sees your data

We use a small number of service providers to run the app:

  • Supabase — stores our database, files, and handles sign-in
  • Apple — Sign in with Apple, and delivery of push notifications to iPhones
  • Google — Sign in with Google; and Google's Places service, which we use to fetch venue photos and addresses (this part does not involve your personal data)
  • Expo — sends push notifications to Apple for delivery to your device
  • Mixpanel — usage analytics
  • Resend — sends account and waitlist emails
  • OpenAI — checks photos you post for objectionable content

Each of these acts on our instructions. We do not sell or rent your information.

All of these providers are United States companies, and your data is stored on servers in the United States. We do not transfer your information to companies outside the United States.

Content moderation

Photos you post — line photos, selfies, stories, and profile photos — are checked automatically for objectionable content. This check is performed by OpenAI's moderation service. The photo is sent for analysis and is not used to train anyone's models.

Text you post — comments, story captions, your name, username, and bio — is checked against our own list of prohibited terms. That check happens on our servers and is not sent to any outside service. Direct messages are not checked.

We do this because we are required to keep objectionable material off the app, and because people in your photos did not choose to be there. You agree to this check when you accept our terms — either when you create an account, or when you are asked to accept updated terms if you already have one. We do not send anything for checking before you have accepted.

If we later add a feature that sends your information to an artificial intelligence service for any other purpose, we will ask your permission first and explain what is being shared.

If you report content, we keep that content while we review it, even if it would normally be deleted on the schedule above. Some reports carry legal obligations to preserve material.

What other people can see

Your profile — name, username, photo, and bio — is visible to other users who are signed in. People using the app without an account see venue information only, never your profile, posts, or photos.

Your posts, line reports, and stories are visible according to where you post them.

Your location at a venue is hidden by default. It is only shown to friends if you turn friend visibility on, and you can hide from specific friends or use Masquerade mode to disappear entirely.

Direct messages are visible to the people in the conversation.

Your date of birth and preferences are never shown to other users.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account from inside the app: Profile → Delete Account.

Your account is deactivated immediately and you become invisible to everyone. After 30 days it is permanently deleted, along with your posts, photos, line reports, check-ins, friendships, points, date of birth, and preferences. Signing in during those 30 days cancels the deletion.

Two things survive. Messages you sent stay visible to the people you sent them to, shown as coming from a deleted user. We do this so the other person's conversation still makes sense. Your name, photo, and username are removed from them. Safety reports — ones you made, and ones made about your content — are kept as a safety record.

Your rights

You can access, correct, or delete your information. Most of this is available in the app; for anything else, contact us at the address below.

Depending on where you live, you may have additional rights — including the right to a copy of your data, and the right to object to certain processing. Contact us and we will respond.

Children

The Move is not intended for anyone under 18. We ask for your date of birth when you first use the app and do not let anyone under 18 continue. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone under that age. If you believe a child has created an account, contact us and we will remove it.

Security

We use industry-standard protections, including encryption in transit and access controls on our database. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.

Changes

If we change this policy, we will update the date at the top and, for significant changes, notify you in the app.
